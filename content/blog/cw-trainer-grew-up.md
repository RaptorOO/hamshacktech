When I first put [CW Trainer](https://hamshacktech.com/cw-trainer/) online, it did exactly two things: **Instant Character Recognition**, where a character plays and you hit the space bar the instant you know it, then type it, and **Code Groups**, where you hear a whole group of characters and type it from memory before the clock runs out.


![The CW Trainer Code Groups tab, with character boxes, Head Copy Mode, Start Session and Walk Mode buttons, tool settings and statistics](/img/blog/cw-trainer-grew-up/cw-trainer-code-groups.png)

Instant Character Recognition has since picked up **Car Mode** and **Walk Mode**: listen-only practice that keeps playing with the screen off, so you can easily get some practice in while taking a walk or running errands.

![The Car Mode panel in CW Trainer, with listen-only options for session length, characters, speed and the gap after each play](/img/blog/cw-trainer-grew-up/cw-trainer-icr-car-mode.png)

I didn't plan most of what came next. You did. Nearly every feature since then started as a request, a suggestion, or a "wouldn't it be great if..." from a student working hard to learn Morse code. That's why, in my opinion, CW Trainer is the most innovative, student-driven Morse code trainer available today. You wrote the to-do list. I just kept tinkering.

Here's where it stands now.

## Call Signs Trainer

![The CW Trainer Call Signs Trainer tab, with Find the Number, Call Sign Builder, Call Sign Simulator and Speed Run modes](/img/blog/cw-trainer-grew-up/cw-trainer-call-signs.png)

Call signs mix letters and numbers in patterns you'll hear on the air every day, and copying them cleanly is a skill all its own. The Call Signs Trainer makes up fresh calls in real US and international formats, so there's no list to memorize, and gives you four ways to practice: **Find the Number**, **Call Sign Builder**, **Call Sign Simulator** (every call at a different speed, like the stations on the bands) and **Speed Run** (get one right and the next is 1 WPM faster; miss and it drops 2 WPM).

## Keyer Practice

![The CW Trainer Keyer Practice tab, with Daily Scales, Copy This and Free Send modes, a Your Own Text button and keyer settings](/img/blog/cw-trainer-grew-up/cw-trainer-keyer.png)

Copying is only half of CW. Keyer Practice gives you a place to work on your sending, so your fist gets the same attention as your ear. Plug in your paddles, straight key or bug through a Vail or VBand adapter (or use the keyboard or on-screen pads) and work through Bob Carter's (WR7Q) Daily Scales, copy-and-send exercises, free sending, or your own text: upload any text file, like the lyrics to American Pie, and practice sending it. Each character turns green or red as you send it, and you get a timing report on your spacing, plus your dah-to-dit ratio if you're on a straight key or bug.

## QSO Practice


This is where it all comes together. QSO Practice lets you practice the flow of a real on-air contact, so your first real QSO won't be the first one you've ever had. The other station sends at your copy speed, you answer with your key, and you can pick a Standard QSO, POTA, SOTA, or a CWops CWT exchange, and choose whether to call CQ yourself or respond to someone else's CQ. It's forgiving like a real operator: if something's missing it'll ask for it with QRZ?, NAME?, RST? or PSE AGN, and you can send AGN or QRS right back. Afterward you see what you copied next to what they sent.

## Test Prep


Test Prep is built for current CW Academy students preparing for the entrance test to Intermediate. If that test is on your calendar, this tool was made with you in mind. The actual test isn't published, so Test Prep copies it as closely as we can, from a classmate who took it: 8 questions, five with two everyday words and three US call signs, each sent once at 25 WPM characters with Farnsworth spacing (about 10 WPM overall). You then have 20 seconds to type what you copied, with five suggested answers for each word and call sign, and each word and call sign is marked right or wrong on its own, out of 13. The pass mark isn't published, so you just see your score. Your scores are saved so you can watch them climb.

## My Course


Pick the course you're currently taking in Settings, and My Course becomes your home base for it. It's still under development: right now it supports CW Academy, showing your class schedule once you enter your first class date and class times, plus a link to the CWops curriculum with each class's homework. LICW support is coming soon.

## Progress


The Progress tab tracks how much time you spend each day on each practice tool, along with some interesting stats on how your Morse code learning is coming along: your day streak, characters copied, accuracy, speed, reaction time, your trouble characters for copying and sending, and your sending speed and letter spacing from the Keyer tab. You can even add practice time from a Zoom class or an on-air session. It's encouraging to see how far you've come, and a nice nudge on days you'd rather skip practice.

## Morse Code Jeopardy


And then there's **multiplayer Morse Code Jeopardy**, which as far as I know is a first. You still get intense copying and sending practice, but in a fun game-show setting with other students and a group chat built right in. Everyone joins a room from their own CW Trainer, hears each clue on their own device at their own speed, and sends the answer with their key. It's deliberately not competitive, since players are at different stages of their Morse journey: the board is solved together for a Group Score, and your own score is visible only to you. Practice stops feeling like homework when you're solving the board with your classmates.
