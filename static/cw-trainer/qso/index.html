    How QSO Practice Works

    Pick the type of contact at the top and who calls CQ, then press Start QSO. Your call sign, name and QTH, and both speeds, are in Settings.

    The other station sends at your copy speed and you answer with your key at your sending speed. They can be different: copy 25 WPM with Farnsworth 12 and send at 15, for example. End each of your overs the usual way — K, KN, BK or SK — then pause, or press Enter. If you stop without one, a few seconds’ pause ends your over.

    It’s forgiving like a real operator: order doesn’t matter, repeats are fine, and a call sign with one letter off still gets through. If something’s missing they’ll ask — QRZ? (who’s calling?), NAME?, RST?, PSE AGN — or QRS if they can’t read you. You can ask them too: send AGN or ? to hear their last over again, or QRS to have them slow down.

    Standard QSO: call signs, signal report, name and QTH, then 73. POTA: the quick Parks on the Air exchange — the activator calls CQ POTA with the park number, hunters send just their call, each side sends 5NN (599) and state, then TU. SOTA: the same idea from a mountain top — the activator signs /P and gives the summit reference; real signal reports are traded. CWT: the CWops Test, a fast weekly mini-contest — the exchange is just name and CWops member number (CWA if you don’t have one), with no signal report. Your number goes in Settings, under Your station.

    When the contact ends, fill in what you copied from them and press Check My Copy. Last Contact shows what you got right, and Your Sending shows your speed and spacing.

    Elapsed counts up from Start QSO. Tap Timer to choose how long to practice; when it runs out a bell rings and the contact ends. Record saves the contact, both stations, as a sound file.
